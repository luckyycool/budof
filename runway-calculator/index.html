The Definitive Founder's Guide to Calculating Startup Runway & Managing Net Burn Rate

Startup runway is the single most vital metric determining whether an early-stage company reaches its next valuation inflection point or faces catastrophic insolvency. In this guide, we break down the exact mathematics, tax burdens, and growth frameworks required to steer your company toward Default Alive status.

1. The Mathematical Runway Formula

At its core, runway measures the number of months a startup can sustain operations before its liquid cash balance drops to zero. While many founders use a simplified estimate, an institutional financial model calculates runway by accounting for dynamic revenue expansion and staged headcount additions:

Basic Net Burn Formula
Net Burn = Gross Operating Expenses (Payroll + Software + Hosting + Rent) − Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)

Dynamic Runway Horizon Formula
Runway (Months) = Total Liquid Cash Balance ÷ Average Monthly Net Burn

When Net Burn is negative (MRR exceeds Gross OpEx), the company generates positive monthly free cash flow. In this scenario, mathematically the company has infinite runway and is Default Alive.

2. Gross Burn vs. Net Burn: Understanding the Critical Difference

Venture capitalists scrutinize both Gross Burn and Net Burn during Series A and Series B due diligence:

  • Gross Burn: The absolute total dollar amount leaving your bank account each month, regardless of incoming customer revenue. If your payroll is $40,000, AWS bill is $5,000, and software SaaS seats are $3,000, your Gross Burn is $48,000/month.
  • Net Burn: The actual deficit between incoming cash collections and gross expenses. If your Gross Burn is $48,000 and your MRR is $28,000, your Net Burn is $20,000/month.

3. The Hidden 15% Employer Payroll Tax & Benefit Overhead

One of the most frequent reasons founders run out of cash 3 to 5 months earlier than projected is failing to model fully-loaded payroll costs. When you hire an engineer at a base salary of $10,000 per month ($120,000/year), your company actually spends approximately $11,500 per month ($138,000/year):

4. Paul Graham's "Default Alive vs. Default Dead" Framework

In his seminal 2015 essay, Y Combinator co-founder Paul Graham urged founders to ask a single, blunt question as soon as they have revenue: "Are we Default Alive or Default Dead?"

✓

Default Alive

At your current month-over-month revenue growth rate, your MRR will surpass your expenses before your cash runs out. You control your own destiny and can negotiate fundraising terms from a position of sovereign strength.

!

Default Dead

Even with historical revenue growth, your cash reserves will reach zero before profitability. Unless you accelerate growth, cut discretionary burn, or secure outside equity investment, the company will dissolve.
